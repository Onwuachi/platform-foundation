# Derrick's 2026 Bourbon Finds

Over the course of 2026, my bourbon journey changed.

I stopped looking at bourbon strictly through the lens of price, reputation, allocated status, or what the internet told me I should be drinking.

Instead, I started paying closer attention to what actually happens in the glass.

I began focusing on the things I personally value:

* Integrated flavor
* Good oak
* Caramel and vanilla
* Fruit that complements rather than dominates
* Balanced spice
* Good mouthfeel
* A finish that invites another sip
* Proof that adds character without simply adding heat
* And above all, value

This page is a running record of the bottles that surprised me, changed my perspective, or reminded me that some of the best bourbon experiences don't require the highest price tag.

These aren't necessarily my **Top 5 bourbons**.

They are the bottles that stood out during my 2026 journey.

Some may eventually make the permanent rankings.

Others may simply remain memorable discoveries.

---

# 🏆 2026 Find of the Year

## Evan Williams Single Barrel — Vintage 2016

**Barrel No. 1307**
**Barreled:** December 6, 2016
**Bottled:** February 10, 2025
**Proof:** 86.6
**Price Paid:** $26.99
**Rating:** **9.6 / 10**

### Why It Made the List

This was the bottle that made me stop and rethink what I thought I knew about Evan Williams Single Barrel.

I had spent a lot of time talking about Heaven Hill 7 Year Bottled-in-Bond and appreciating what it brought to the glass.

Then I opened this.

And everything suddenly clicked.

This particular vintage was nearly ten years old when bottled, and the maturity was immediately obvious.

The whiskey was incredibly integrated.

Caramel.

Vanilla.

Sweet cherry.

Floral notes.

Light pepper.

Everything worked together rather than competing for attention.

The lower 86.6 proof wasn't a weakness. In this case, it may have been part of the reason the whiskey was so approachable and balanced.

The oak was present without becoming bitter or drying.

The sweetness was rich without becoming simple.

The fruit was prominent without becoming overly grapey.

The mouthfeel was creamy, and the heat built gradually rather than attacking the palate.

The finish left behind creamy vanilla and sweet fruit and, most importantly, invited another sip.

This was a bottle that I could simply keep drinking.

And at **$26.99**, the value was almost absurd.

### The Moment

This bottle completely changed how I looked at the lower-proof Evan Williams Single Barrel releases.

I had been willing to spend significantly more on bottles because I assumed the additional proof, age, or reputation would automatically translate into a better experience.

This bottle reminded me that bourbon doesn't work that way.

A well-aged, well-integrated whiskey at 86.6 proof can be more enjoyable than a much more expensive bottle.

The key isn't simply proof.

It isn't simply age.

It's how everything comes together.

### Flavor Profile

**Nose**

* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Sweet cherry
* Floral notes
* Light oak

**Palate**

* Caramel
* Toffee
* Vanilla
* Sweet cherry
* Light pepper
* Creamy texture

**Finish**

* Creamy vanilla
* Sweet fruit
* Gentle oak
* Gradually fading spice

### Comparison

This reminded me of a bourbon that could sit somewhere between the approachable sweetness of a well-aged Heaven Hill bourbon and the refined fruit character associated with older bourbons.

It even gave me a faint comparison to Eagle Rare in the way the fruit presents itself, but without the grape-forward character I sometimes associate with Eagle Rare.

For me, this was more balanced.

### Final Verdict

This is my **2026 Bourbon Find of the Year** so far.

A nearly ten-year-old Evan Williams Single Barrel that cost $26.99 and delivered an experience I would happily put against bottles costing considerably more.

The crazy part?

I had two bottles from this vintage available to me.

I bought both.

I should have bought the last one.

I left it behind thinking someone else deserved the opportunity to find it.

That bottle is now gone.

The lesson has been learned.

When the bourbon is this good and the price is this right, don't overthink it.

**2026 Find Rating: 9.6 / 10**

**Status:** 🏆 Current 2026 Find of the Year

# 🥈 Old Grand-Dad 114

**Proof:** 114
**Price Paid:** $25.99
**Rating:** **9.0 / 10**

### Why It Made the List

Old Grand-Dad 114 is another bottle that changed with me.

Years ago, around 2012, I didn't appreciate it the way I do today.

Now?

My palate has changed.

After spending more time exploring bourbon, I have come back around to the high-rye spice and bold character that make Old Grand-Dad 114 unique.

The first thing that still gets me is the color.

Deep, dark amber with a reddish cast that absolutely shines in the glass.

This is one of those bottles where the appearance alone gets your attention.

### What I Found

The bourbon opens with a combination of:

* Deep cherry
* Oak
* Vanilla
* Caramel
* Leather
* High-rye spice

The spice is immediately more prominent than something like Wild Turkey Rare Breed.

But it isn't simply hot.

It develops gradually and lingers before eventually tapering away.

There is a depth to the bourbon that makes it far more interesting than its price suggests.

It doesn't have the unmistakable Turkey funk of Rare Breed, and it isn't as refined as Russell's Reserve Single Barrel.

But it doesn't need to be.

At $25.99, Old Grand-Dad 114 is doing something different.

And it's doing it extremely well.

### Comparison

Compared with **Wild Turkey Rare Breed**, OGD 114 is:

* More spice-forward
* More high-rye driven
* Less "Turkey funk"
* Still rich with cherry, oak, vanilla, and caramel

Compared with **Russell's Reserve Single Barrel**, it lacks some of the maturity and refinement.

But the value proposition is completely different.

This is the bottle that makes you question why you need to spend $50, $60, or $70 for a high-proof bourbon.

### Final Verdict

For me, Old Grand-Dad 114 is currently the **king of the above-110-proof value category**.

It may not beat Rare Breed or Russell's Single Barrel on absolute refinement.

But at **$25.99**, it doesn't have to.

This is a no-brainer bottle for anyone who enjoys spice, oak, and high-proof bourbon.

**2026 Find Rating: 9.0 / 10**

**Status:** 👑 High-Proof Value King

# 🥉 Center Stave Port Cask Finished Bourbon — Cask Strength

**Proof:** 108.2
**Price Paid:** $44.99
**Rating:** **8.8 / 10**

### Why It Made the List

This bottle surprised me for a completely different reason.

Port-finished bourbon can easily become too wine-forward.

That was my biggest concern.

Instead, the port influence was remarkably integrated.

The wine character never took over the bourbon.

The bourbon still tasted like bourbon.

### What I Found

**Nose**

* Integrated fruit
* Oak
* Vanilla
* Subtle wine influence

The proof was surprisingly well hidden.

It drank more like an older 8–10 year bourbon than a cask-strength release.

**Palate**

* Creamy texture
* Balanced heat
* Pepper
* Oily oak
* Cherry
* Caramel

The pepper provided structure without overwhelming the palate.

The cherry leaned more toward mature bourbon fruit than obvious wine.

**Finish**

Long and sweet.

Less dry than expected.

The sweetness reminded me more of the way Wild Turkey Rare Breed finishes than a typical wine-finished bourbon.

### Comparison

This bottle surprised me because it landed closer to familiar bourbon territory than I expected.

I could easily compare aspects of it to:

* Heaven Hill Bottled-in-Bond
* Bardstown Bottled-in-Bond
* Bondstone

The port cask finish added complexity without turning the bourbon into a wine experiment.

### Final Verdict

This was a very good buy.

The biggest surprise was how balanced it remained.

It proved that a port-finished bourbon doesn't have to be dominated by the finishing barrel.

**2026 Find Rating: 8.8 / 10**

**Status:** 🍷 Best Port-Finished Surprise

# What 2026 Taught Me

The biggest lesson of my 2026 bourbon journey is simple:

**The best bourbon isn't necessarily the most expensive bourbon.**

I've watched bottles I genuinely enjoy climb in price.

Wild Turkey Rare Breed.

Old Forester 1920.

Old Forester 1910.

Even bottles that once represented obvious value are increasingly being positioned as premium products.

That has changed how I shop.

I'm no longer willing to automatically pay more just because a bottle has a bigger name.

Instead, I'm asking:

**Does this bourbon actually taste better to me?**

That's how a $26.99 bottle like Evan Williams Single Barrel Vintage 2016 can become more exciting than a $50+ bottle.

That's how Old Grand-Dad 114 can compete with bourbon costing twice as much.

That's how a $44.99 port-finished bourbon can surprise me.

And that's why I'm becoming more selective.

I would rather buy a bottle I genuinely love for $25–$40 than spend $60 simply because the market says I should.

But reputation is not quality.
