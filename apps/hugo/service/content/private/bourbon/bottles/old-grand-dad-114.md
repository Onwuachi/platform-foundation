# Why I Bought It

Old Grand-Dad 114 is a bottle I have come back to after many years.

I remember trying it around **2012** and not being particularly impressed. My palate was different then, and this was not a bourbon I expected to revisit.

Years later, after spending more time exploring bourbon and developing a greater appreciation for spice, oak, and higher-proof whiskey, I decided to give it another look.

This time, I found something very different.

Or perhaps more accurately, I found something that my palate was finally ready to appreciate.

I paid **$25.99** for this bottle, and at that price, Old Grand-Dad 114 may be the best high-proof value bourbon I have tasted in years.

This isn't about buying bourbon because it's cheap.

It's about recognizing when a bourbon delivers far more character than its price suggests.

And Old Grand-Dad 114 delivers.

# Appearance

The color is one of the first things that always impresses me about Old Grand-Dad 114.

Deep, dark amber with a striking reddish cast.

In the glass, it looks considerably more expensive than the price tag suggests.

The rich color immediately sets the expectation for a bourbon with plenty of oak and depth.

---

# Nose

The nose leans firmly into the high-rye character.

There is noticeable spice, but underneath it are classic bourbon notes of:

- Vanilla
- Caramel
- Deep cherry
- Oak
- Leather

The profile has a rustic quality that feels distinctly Jim Beam.

It isn't as polished or refined as some of the more expensive bourbons on my shelf, but that isn't necessarily a weakness.

Old Grand-Dad 114 has personality.

---

# Palate

The palate is where this bottle really won me over.

The first thing that stands out is the combination of **proof and spice**.

At 114 proof, it has plenty of intensity, but the heat doesn't completely overwhelm the flavor.

I get:

- Deep cherry
- Oak
- Vanilla
- Caramel
- Leather
- Pronounced baking spice
- A gradually building peppery character

The cherry, oak, vanilla, and caramel integrate surprisingly well with the spice.

The spice is more prominent than what I find in many of my favorite bourbons, making this a very different experience from something like Heaven Hill Bottled-in-Bond.

There is also a rustic leather quality that adds depth.

The proof provides plenty of weight without turning the bourbon into a simple alcohol bomb.

---

# Finish

The finish is long and warming.

The spice gradually lingers before slowly tapering away.

Oak, leather, and sweetness remain underneath the spice, keeping the finish balanced rather than allowing the peppery character to completely dominate.

Compared with Wild Turkey Rare Breed, the finish is less funky and less distinctly "Turkey."

It doesn't have the same Wild Turkey character that makes Rare Breed and Russell's Reserve so recognizable.

Instead, Old Grand-Dad 114 offers a more direct expression of high-rye bourbon spice layered over deep cherry, oak, vanilla, and caramel.

---

# Overall Impression

Old Grand-Dad 114 completely changed my opinion of the bottle.

I used to overlook it.

Now, I consider it one of the best high-proof value bourbons available.

This is not Russell's Reserve Single Barrel.

It is not Wild Turkey Rare Breed.

It doesn't have the same refinement, depth, or distinctive Turkey profile.

But at **$25.99**, it doesn't need to.

Old Grand-Dad 114 delivers enough proof, spice, oak, fruit, and bourbon character to compete with bottles that cost considerably more.

For me, this is now the **above-110-proof value king**.

# What I'd Change

Honestly, very little at this price.

If I were comparing it directly to more expensive bourbons, I would want more refinement and integration.

The profile is more rustic than something like Russell's Reserve Single Barrel.

It also lacks the distinctive "Turkey funk" I enjoy in Wild Turkey Rare Breed.

But those aren't really flaws.

They're simply differences in style.

At **$25.99**, I wouldn't change much about this bourbon.

# Comparison Notes

## Wild Turkey Rare Breed

Rare Breed is more refined and carries a distinctive Wild Turkey character that Old Grand-Dad 114 doesn't replicate.

Rare Breed has more of that classic Turkey funk and a deeper sense of integration.

Old Grand-Dad 114, however, brings more obvious spice and costs dramatically less.

If I want the more polished experience, I reach for Rare Breed.

If I want an affordable, high-proof bourbon with plenty of spice and character, Old Grand-Dad 114 is an easy choice.

## Russell's Reserve Single Barrel

Russell's Reserve Single Barrel offers more maturity, refinement, and depth.

Old Grand-Dad 114 is rougher around the edges but delivers an impressive amount of character for the price.

They are not substitutes, but they occupy some similar territory for me as bold, higher-proof bourbons.

## Heaven Hill Bottled-in-Bond

Heaven Hill Bottled-in-Bond is more balanced and approachable, with the classic Heaven Hill profile that I enjoy so much.

Old Grand-Dad 114 is more aggressive and spice-forward.

If I want a smoother, more balanced pour, Heaven Hill BiB wins.

If I want spice and intensity, Old Grand-Dad 114 gets the call.
