# Why I Bought It

I wanted to try Early Times Bottled in Bond for myself.

There's a lot of love for Early Times Bottled in Bond among bourbon drinkers, particularly for the version produced under Brown-Forman.

But this isn't that whiskey.

The Early Times brand was acquired by **Sazerac in 2020**, and production of the current bourbon moved to **Barton 1792 Distillery**.

So I wasn't buying this expecting to discover the legendary old Early Times.

I wanted to taste the current version and decide for myself.

At **$23.99 for a 1-liter bottle**, it was inexpensive enough to make the experiment worthwhile.

And honestly?

I probably should have stayed with **Evan Williams Bottled in Bond**.

# A Note About the "Old" Early Times

This is important when reviewing Early Times.

The Early Times brand has a long history.

Brown-Forman acquired the brand in 1923, and Early Times Bottled in Bond eventually became an iconic bourbon label.

However, the brand changed significantly over the decades.

In 2020, Sazerac acquired the Early Times brand from Brown-Forman.

Production of the bourbon subsequently moved to **Barton 1792 Distillery**, with Sazerac stating that the original mash bill and proprietary yeast strain would continue.

That history is part of what makes this bottle interesting.

But I'm not going to give the current bottle extra points because of the reputation of the old one.

I'm reviewing **what is actually in my glass**.

And this doesn't taste like the Early Times Bottled in Bond that people describe when they're talking about the highly sought-after older Brown-Forman-era bottles.

---

# Appearance

The whiskey is a **golden amber**.

It's noticeably lighter than some of the darker, more heavily oak-driven bourbons in my collection.

The color fits what I'm experiencing in the glass.

There is some barrel influence, but this isn't presenting as a heavily matured oak bomb.

---

# Nose

The nose is surprisingly floral.

I get:

* Light floral notes
* Sweet corn
* Vanilla
* Light caramel
* Grain
* Gentle oak

There's a noticeable corn character that reminds me somewhat of **Mellow Corn**.

It's not the same whiskey and the mash bills are very different, but the grain-forward character is something I notice.

The nose is pleasant, but it doesn't immediately suggest a deeply mature bourbon.

---

# Palate

The palate is where the bottle becomes more complicated for me.

The first thing I notice is the **corn sweetness**.

There is:

* Corn
* Vanilla
* Caramel
* Light brown sugar
* Oak
* Pepper spice
* Grain sweetness

But then the spice arrives.

And it sticks around.

This bourbon has a **higher pepper spice presence** than I prefer.

It's not completely out of balance, but the pepper makes the whiskey feel hotter than I expected from the overall flavor profile.

There is also a funky oak character that reminds me somewhat of **Wild Turkey**.

That's an interesting note because I generally like that Wild Turkey character.

Here, however, it isn't as well integrated into the overall profile.

---

# The Spice

This is probably the biggest deciding factor in the 7.0 rating.

The pepper spice isn't necessarily bad.

In fact, if you really enjoy rye-forward or peppery bourbon, you may find more to appreciate here than I did.

For my palate, though, it keeps the bottle from becoming an easy pour.

The whiskey continues changing in the glass, and as it warms up I can find more of the underlying character.

But I have to be willing to deal with that persistent pepper.

That's the tradeoff.

If you can handle the spice, there's more here to explore.

If you want a softer, sweeter Bottled-in-Bond experience, I would reach for something else.

---

# Oak

The oak is a mixed bag.

There is enough oak to provide structure, but I also pick up a **slightly bitter oak character** toward the later part of the palate and finish.

This isn't the pleasant mature oak I get from something like Russell's Reserve Single Barrel.

It's a little more bitter and less integrated.

That contributes to the feeling that the whiskey is still trying to find its balance.

---

# Finish

The finish is medium.

Pepper spice continues to linger, followed by oak and corn sweetness.

The bitter oak note becomes more noticeable toward the end.

There is still some sweetness underneath everything, but the spice and oak are what stay with me longest.

The finish isn't terrible.

It's just not particularly elegant.

---

# Mouthfeel

Medium-bodied.

At **100 proof**, I expected a little more weight.

Instead, it drinks somewhat lighter than the proof suggests.

That's not necessarily a negative.

The whiskey remains easy enough to sip, but the persistent pepper makes it feel hotter than some other 100-proof bourbons I enjoy.

---

# Overall Impression

This is where I land:

**I probably should have stayed with Evan Williams Bottled in Bond.**

EW BiB gives me more of what I personally want from a $20-something Bottled-in-Bond bourbon:

* Sweetness
* Vanilla
* Caramel
* Oak
* Balance
* Easy drinkability
* Excellent value

Early Times gives me something different.

It is more corn-forward to my palate, more floral on the nose, and carries a persistent pepper spice that changes the drinking experience.

There's also a funky oak character that reminds me of Wild Turkey.

I actually find that interesting.

The problem is that the individual pieces don't integrate as well as I would like.

The whiskey keeps changing in the glass, and if I slow down and work through it, there are things worth finding.

But I shouldn't have to work quite this hard for a bottle I'm comparing against EW BiB.

## Early Times BiB vs. Mellow Corn

There is an interesting grain connection here.

Mellow Corn is much more explicitly corn-driven because it is a straight corn whiskey.

Early Times is still bourbon, with a mash bill of:

**79% corn / 11% rye / 10% malted barley.**

Yet I still perceive a corn-forward character in Early Times that reminds me of Mellow Corn.

Mellow Corn is:

* Sweeter
* Creamier
* More obviously corn-driven
* More relaxed
* More approachable

Early Times is:

* More peppery
* More oak-driven
* More bourbon-like
* More floral
* More challenging

Mellow Corn is actually the bottle I find myself enjoying more despite costing less.

# Value

This is where Early Times gets some credit.

**$23.99 for a liter of 100-proof Bottled-in-Bond bourbon** is not a terrible deal.

That's a lot of whiskey for the money.

The problem is that value isn't the only thing I'm evaluating anymore.

I've reached the point in my bourbon journey where I would rather spend a few more dollars on a bottle I genuinely prefer than buy something simply because the bottle is cheap.

That's exactly why EW BiB remains the better choice for me.

---

It means my palate has become more selective.

---

# Final Thoughts

Early Times Bottled in Bond is an interesting bourbon precisely because of what it isn't.

It isn't the old Early Times Bottled in Bond that bourbon enthusiasts often talk about.

It's the **current Sazerac-era version**, produced at Barton 1792 Distillery.

And judging this bottle on its own merits, I find it decent but not exceptional.

The whiskey is golden amber with a floral nose and a noticeable corn character.

The palate brings vanilla, caramel, corn, oak, and pepper.

The pepper spice is the defining characteristic for me.

If you can handle the slightly higher pepper spice, the whiskey becomes more interesting as it sits in the glass and warms up.

There is also a funky oak character that reminds me somewhat of Wild Turkey.

But alongside that is a slight bitter oak note that keeps the profile from becoming truly cohesive.

For **$23.99 per liter**, it's not the worst purchase I've made.

Actually, it's a pretty reasonable experiment.

But I know what I would rather drink.

**Evan Williams Bottled in Bond.**

That's the lesson from this bottle.

I'm not looking for the cheapest whiskey anymore.

I'm looking for the whiskey that gives me the experience I actually want.

Early Times BiB is worth trying.

It just isn't worth chasing.
