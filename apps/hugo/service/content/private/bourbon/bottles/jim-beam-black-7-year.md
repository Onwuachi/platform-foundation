# Why I Bought It

I've never considered myself much of a Jim Beam fan.

Most standard Jim Beam expressions have always struck me as younger, grain-forward bourbons that lacked the depth I usually look for. The 2024 relaunch of Jim Beam Black with a guaranteed 7-year age statement changed that equation.

At under $25, I wanted to see whether additional maturity could elevate the familiar Beam profile.

# Appearance

Dark golden brown with a rich amber glow.

Darker than standard Jim Beam and showing noticeably more maturity in the glass.

---

# Nose

The aroma immediately delivers exactly what Jim Beam promised.

- Caramel
- Vanilla
- Fresh oak
- Black pepper
- Roasted malt

Everything is well integrated without feeling youthful or overly grain-driven.

---

# Palate

The oak arrives first.

Not overly tannic or bitter, but sitting comfortably between fresh sweet oak and older leather-like oak.

The spice builds gradually with warming pepper that never becomes aggressive.

Vanilla weaves throughout the palate while caramel and hard candy sweetness balance the oak beautifully.

I also pick up subtle herbal notes, though nothing approaching the mint or eucalyptus character found in bottles like Baby Sazerac.

The whiskey drinks considerably older than its price suggests.

---

# Finish

Medium-long.

The oak slowly fades while vanilla, caramel, and lingering baking spice remain.

Heat is present but controlled, making this an easy sipping bourbon despite its flavorful profile.

---

# Overall Impression

This is not the Jim Beam Black I remember.

The additional maturity transforms the profile into something that begins leaning toward Knob Creek territory while maintaining its own identity.

It reminds me of a midpoint between:

- Knob Creek 9 Year
- Knob Creek 12 Year
- Knob Creek Blender's Select Batch 01

Not because it reaches their overall depth, but because the oak maturity and integrated spice finally allow the Beam mashbill to shine.

At this price, it becomes one of the stronger value bourbons on the shelf.

---

# What I Liked

- Excellent value under $25
- Seven years of age is noticeable
- Oak is mature without bitterness
- Vanilla and caramel remain balanced
- Pepper spice is integrated
- Drinks older than expected
- Easy daily sipper

---

# What I'd Change

At 90 proof, I can't help but wonder what this profile would become at bottled-in-bond or barrel strength.

The foundation is excellent and feels capable of carrying considerably more proof.

---

# Comparison Notes

Closest bottles in my collection:

- Knob Creek 9 Year
- Knob Creek 12 Year
- Knob Creek Blender's Select Batch 01

It doesn't replace those bottles, but it surprisingly shares portions of their mature Beam oak profile.

If someone enjoys Knob Creek but wants an everyday bottle at roughly half the price, Jim Beam Black 7 Year deserves serious consideration.

# Final Thoughts

Jim Beam Black's return to a guaranteed seven-year age statement was more than a marketing refresh—it materially improved the whiskey.

The extra maturation rounds off the youthful edges found in standard Jim Beam while allowing caramel, vanilla, pepper, and mature oak to develop into a balanced, approachable bourbon.

It won't replace Knob Creek 12 on my shelf, but considering it costs roughly one-third as much, that's hardly a criticism.

For around $23, this is one of the strongest everyday pours in the Beam portfolio.
