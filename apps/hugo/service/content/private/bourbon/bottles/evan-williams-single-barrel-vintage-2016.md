Sometimes the best bourbon finds aren't the bottles everyone is chasing.

# Appearance

The color immediately caught my attention.

In the Glencairn glass, this bourbon appears as a **light golden amber** that almost seems to glow.

It isn't the deep, dark amber of a high-proof barrel-proof bourbon.

Instead, it has a lighter, polished appearance that fits the lower proof.

But don't let the lighter color fool you.

There is a maturity and depth here that the appearance doesn't fully reveal.

---

# Nose

The nose is remarkably integrated.

There is no single note that dominates.

Instead, the bourbon presents a beautifully connected combination of:

* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Sweet cherry
* Light oak
* Floral notes
* Gentle pepper
* Sweet fruit

The first thing I noticed was how **mature and integrated** the whiskey smelled.

There is no obvious alcohol blast.

The 86.6 proof is extremely well hidden.

If I tasted this blind, I would have difficulty guessing that it was bottled at such a modest proof.

The overall impression is closer to a well-aged bourbon than a typical lower-proof shelf bottle.

---

# Palate

This is where the bottle completely won me over.

The palate is incredibly creamy and balanced.

The bourbon opens with:

* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Toffee
* Sweet cherry
* Light pepper
* Soft oak

The cherry sweetness is particularly impressive.

It isn't artificial or candy-like.

It is the kind of deep, naturally sweet cherry character that makes me think of some of the more mature bourbons I've enjoyed.

The oak is present but beautifully controlled.

Nothing feels overdone.

The heat builds gradually rather than arriving as a sharp punch.

There is just enough pepper and spice to keep the bourbon interesting without overpowering the sweeter flavors.

The result is an incredibly approachable bourbon that somehow manages to be both **sweet and complex**.

And the most impressive part?

The proof is almost completely hidden.

At 86.6 proof, I expected something lighter and perhaps thinner.

Instead, this bourbon delivers a creamy, satisfying texture with enough flavor to keep inviting another sip.

And then another.

---

# Finish

The finish is where the integration really becomes apparent.

It starts with lingering:

* Vanilla
* Sweet cherry
* Caramel
* Soft oak
* Gentle pepper

The sweetness stays with you while the spice gradually rises and then slowly tapers away.

There is no aggressive burn.

Instead, the finish remains creamy and softly warming.

The final impression is a combination of **vanilla, sweet fruit, light oak, and gentle spice**.

It is an exceptionally easy bourbon to keep sipping.

---

# Overall Impression

This bottle completely changed the way I think about Evan Williams Single Barrel.

I've spent a lot of time during this bourbon journey talking about Heaven Hill 7 Year Bottled-in-Bond.

I still love that bourbon.

But this bottle showed me something important.

**Proof and price do not automatically determine enjoyment.**

At only 86.6 proof and $26.99, this particular vintage delivers an experience that I would happily put alongside significantly more expensive bourbons.

It is incredibly integrated.

It is sweet without being overly sweet.

It has oak without becoming dry or tannic.

It has spice without becoming aggressive.

It has floral character without becoming delicate.

And it has enough maturity to make the entire experience feel far more expensive than it actually is.

This is the kind of bourbon that makes the bourbon hunt worthwhile.

# Comparison Notes

## Compared to Heaven Hill 7 Year Bottled-in-Bond

This is the comparison that surprised me most.

I've spent a significant amount of this bourbon journey appreciating **Heaven Hill 7 Year Bottled-in-Bond** as one of my benchmark bourbons.

At 100 proof, Heaven Hill 7 Year brings more power, oak, and bourbon intensity.

But this Evan Williams Single Barrel is considerably more relaxed.

The lower proof allows the sweetness and fruit to shine.

I find more:

* Sweet cherry
* Floral character
* Vanilla
* Creaminess

The Heaven Hill 7 Year feels more substantial and powerful.

This Evan Williams feels more elegant and effortless.

If I want a 100-proof, more assertive pour, I reach for Heaven Hill 7 Year.

If I want something incredibly easy to sip and beautifully integrated, this bottle may actually be the better experience.

That is a shocking statement considering the price difference.

---

## Compared to Eagle Rare

There is a faint connection in the sweet cherry and oak character that reminds me of Eagle Rare.

But this Evan Williams Single Barrel does something I personally prefer.

The cherry is present without becoming overly grape-forward.

The overall profile feels more balanced between:

* Cherry
* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Oak
* Floral notes

It gives me some of the qualities I enjoy in Eagle Rare while avoiding the stronger grape character I sometimes associate with it.

---

## Compared to Wild Turkey Rare Breed

Rare Breed is a completely different animal.

Rare Breed brings:

* Higher proof
* More intensity
* More spice
* More oak
* Wild Turkey's distinctive character

This Evan Williams is much softer and more integrated.

Rare Breed is the bourbon I reach for when I want intensity.

This is the bourbon I reach for when I want to slow down and enjoy the whiskey itself.

The two are not competitors in the traditional sense.

They represent two different expressions of what great bourbon can be.
