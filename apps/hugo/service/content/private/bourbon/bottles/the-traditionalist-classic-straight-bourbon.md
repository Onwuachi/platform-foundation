# About The Traditionalist

The Traditionalist is a Minnesota bourbon distilled and bottled by **Four Daughters Vineyard & Winery** in **Spring Valley, Minnesota**.

The whiskey comes from a family farming background with deep agricultural roots in southeastern Minnesota.

That connection between farming and whiskey is particularly fitting for a bourbon built around a high-corn mash bill.

The Traditionalist Classic Straight Bourbon uses a distinctive mash bill of:

* **65% Corn**
* **19% Rye**
* **16% Toasted Oats**

The toasted oats are what immediately separate this bourbon from many of the more conventional corn-and-rye mash bills on my shelf.

They don't turn the whiskey into something completely different.

Instead, they add another layer to the grain profile and contribute to the whiskey's overall character and texture.

At a minimum of four years old and bottled at **92 proof**, The Traditionalist sits in a comfortable range for everyday bourbon drinking.

---

# Appearance

A classic bourbon appearance with a warm amber color.

It doesn't present as an extremely dark or heavily oak-driven whiskey, but it has enough color to suggest meaningful barrel influence.

The appearance fits the profile well: approachable, traditional, and mature enough to have developed beyond a young grain-forward spirit.

---

# Nose

The nose is inviting and very much in the bourbon family.

I pick up:

* Brown sugar
* Vanilla
* Honey
* Oats
* Toasted grain
* Light baking spice

The toasted oats add an interesting cereal-like character that separates this from a more conventional bourbon.

There is also a sweetness that reminds me of honey granola.

The overall nose is approachable and pleasant without being overly aggressive.

---

# Palate

The palate starts with a familiar bourbon sweetness.

I get:

* Brown sugar
* Vanilla
* Caramel
* Toasted oats
* Light cherry
* Oak
* Baking spice

The toasted oats provide a subtle difference in the background.

They don't turn this into an oat whiskey, but they add a grain character that gives the bourbon some individuality.

The spice is more noticeable here than I initially expected.

This bourbon runs a little hotter in the spice department than I prefer.

However, the spice is still **well integrated**.

It doesn't overwhelm the sweetness or turn harsh.

The **oak and cherry** work particularly well together.

The cherry provides some fruit character while the oak gives the whiskey structure.

Together, they keep the palate interesting and prevent the bourbon from feeling one-dimensional.

---

# Finish

The finish is medium length.

The spice continues to linger after the initial sweetness fades.

There is also a combination of oak and cherry that remains present toward the end.

The finish is slightly hotter than some of my preferred bourbons, but the heat is manageable.

The oak remains integrated rather than becoming bitter or overly drying.

---

# Mouthfeel

Medium-bodied with a pleasant texture.

The toasted oats contribute to the overall grain character and help give the bourbon some additional texture.

It isn't as rich or viscous as something like Old Forester 1920, Wild Turkey Rare Breed, or a barrel-proof bourbon.

But for a 92-proof whiskey, it has enough body to remain satisfying.

---

# Overall Impression

The Traditionalist Classic Straight Bourbon is a good example of why I enjoy exploring bourbon beyond the major Kentucky names.

This is a **Minnesota bourbon**, distilled and bottled by **Four Daughters Vineyard & Winery in Spring Valley**.

I want to support that.

But more importantly, I don't feel like I have to make excuses for it because it's local.

This is a good bourbon.

It runs a little hotter in spice than I prefer, but that spice remains well integrated with the oak and fruit character.

The combination of **oak and cherry** is probably the part of the profile that stands out most to me.

The toasted oats also give the whiskey a point of differentiation without becoming gimmicky.

At 92 proof, it sits in a comfortable range for everyday drinking.

It isn't going to compete with my highest-rated barrel-proof bottles, but it doesn't need to.

This is a solid, approachable Minnesota bourbon that earns its place on the shelf on its own merits.

# What I'd Change

The main thing I would change is the heat from the spice.

It's not excessive, and it is well integrated, but it is more noticeable than I prefer.

A little more integration between the spice and sweetness would push this closer to the profile I personally enjoy.

I would also be interested in seeing what additional age or proof could do with this mash bill.

The combination of corn, rye, and toasted oats has enough character that I think the whiskey could become even more interesting with additional maturation.
