# Why I Bought It

Maximus has been a long-time go-to beer for me.

I've always considered myself more of a Belgian beer and Imperial Stout guy, but over time I've come to appreciate that a well-made Double IPA can be one of the best everyday craft beer styles.

For me, Maximus represents what I want from a DIPA.

It has the hops, but it doesn't feel like the beer is trying to prove how many IBUs it can pack into a glass. Instead, it has actual body behind the hops.

That balance is what keeps bringing me back.

I picked up a 12-pack for $14.99 at Total Wine, which makes this an especially easy beer to keep around.

# Appearance

Deep golden to amber.

Maximus pours with a richer color than many modern hazy or heavily pale Double IPAs.

The appearance immediately suggests that there is more malt presence behind the hops.

---

# Aroma

Pine and citrus hops lead the way.

There is also a noticeable malt sweetness underneath, giving the aroma more depth than a purely hop-driven IPA.

The overall impression is classic West Coast craft beer:

Hops first.

Malt underneath.

No unnecessary gimmicks.

---

# Flavor

This is where Maximus really separates itself for me.

The first impression is a combination of:

- Citrus
- Pine
- Sweet malt
- Light honey-like sweetness
- Strong but integrated hop character

The hops are assertive, but the beer doesn't become a bitterness competition.

Instead, the malt backbone gives the hops something to work with.

There is a sweetness that reminds me slightly of light honey, but it never becomes dessert-like or cloying.

The result is a beer that feels substantial.

It has flavor.

It has body.

It has hops.

But it doesn't lose its balance.

---

# Mouthfeel

Creamy.

Malty.

Full-bodied.

This is probably the biggest reason I continue to come back to Maximus.

I've realized over time that I don't necessarily want the highest possible IBU in an IPA.

I want **body**.

I want the beer to have enough malt character and texture that the hops feel integrated into the beer rather than simply sitting on top of it.

Maximus delivers that.

It has a satisfying weight without becoming heavy like an Imperial Stout.

---

# Finish

The finish carries the hop character forward with lingering citrus and pine.

There is bitterness, but it remains controlled.

The malt sweetness helps round out the finish, preventing the beer from becoming aggressively bitter or drying out too quickly.

The result is a finish that makes you want another sip.

And then another.

---

# What I Liked

- Excellent balance between hops and malt.
- Creamy and substantial mouthfeel.
- Strong body for a Double IPA.
- Citrus and pine hop character.
- Light honey-like malt sweetness.
- 9% ABV without feeling overly hot.
- 60 IBUs that feel integrated rather than aggressive.
- Doesn't chase the modern "maximum hops at all costs" approach.
- Classic Lagunitas character.
- Excellent value at $14.99 for a 12-pack.

Most importantly:

**This beer has personality without being pretentious.**

It doesn't need to be the most extreme IPA in the room.

It just needs to be a damn good beer.

---

# What I'd Change

Honestly, not much.

If anything, I could see wanting just a little more complexity as I drink through it.

But that's a minor criticism.

Maximus already does what I want a Double IPA to do:

Give me hops.

Give me malt.

Give me body.

Keep it balanced.

# Comparison Notes

## Hop Stoopid

Hop Stoopid is a fantastic IPA in its own right, but it feels more hop-forward to me.

It brings:

- More immediate hop impact
- Tropical fruit
- Pine
- A more noticeable bitterness

Maximus feels more substantial.

It has more body and a stronger malt backbone, which makes it the beer I would reach for when I want a more complete and rounded DIPA experience.

Hop Stoopid is the more aggressive hop expression.

Maximus is the more balanced and substantial beer.

---

## My Broader Beer Preference

I've always considered myself a Belgian beer and Imperial Stout guy.

Those styles still hold a special place in my collection.

But as my palate has evolved, I've realized that a **well-made standard Double IPA may be one of the best places for me**.

I don't need an IPA with absurd IBUs.

I don't need a hazy beer overloaded with adjuncts.

I don't need the beer to scream "LOOK HOW HOPPY I AM."

I want:

**Body.**

**Malt.**

**Hops.**

**Balance.**

Maximus hits that combination extremely well.

# Final Thoughts

Maximus is one of those beers that reminds me why I love craft beer.

I pride myself on being a Belgian and Imperial Stout guy.

Those styles still represent a big part of my palate.

But over the years I've realized that sometimes a great Double IPA is exactly where it's at.

Maximus doesn't try to overwhelm me with bitterness.

It doesn't feel pretentious.

It doesn't need to be the most extreme beer ever brewed.

It is simply a damn good Double IPA with:

- Creamy body
- Strong malt backbone
- Citrus and pine hops
- Light honey-like sweetness
- Integrated bitterness
- Excellent drinkability

The combination of malt and hops is what makes this beer work for me.

It's a beer I can drink and immediately understand why I keep coming back to it.

And at $14.99 for a 12-pack?

That's an easy yes.
