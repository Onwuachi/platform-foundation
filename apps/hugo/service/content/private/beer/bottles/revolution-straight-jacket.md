# Why I Bought It

Straight Jacket is one of my favorite high-ABV beers and a beer I return to specifically for its ability to deliver a rich, bourbon-influenced drinking experience without becoming simply a boozy beer.

At 15% ABV, this is not a beer to drink quickly. It is a deliberate sipper that rewards slowing down and allowing the beer to change as it opens.

The combination of caramel, vanilla, sweet malt, dark fruit, and subtle dark oak puts it very close to the sensory territory I enjoy in bourbon.

# Appearance

**Dark red-amber** with a deep, rich appearance.

The color immediately suggests the malt intensity and barrel character of the beer.

---

# Aroma

The aroma is rich and sweet with:

* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Sweet malt
* Dark fruit
* Subtle oak

The bourbon-barrel influence is present without overwhelming the underlying barleywine character.

---

# Flavor

This is a **high-ABV sipper** that does a remarkably good job of hiding its strength.

The first impression is dominated by:

* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Sweet malt
* Dark fruit

The 15% ABV is definitely there, but it doesn't come across as harsh or aggressively alcoholic.

As the beer opens up, a **hidden dark-oak character** begins to emerge underneath the sweetness. It isn't obvious, dry oak sitting on top of the beer. Instead, it feels deeper and darker, adding another layer beneath the caramel and vanilla.

The result is a rich barleywine that feels almost bourbon-like in its progression.

---

# Mouthfeel

**Creamy, full-bodied, and smooth.**

The texture is one of the strongest parts of the beer. It has the weight expected from a 15% barleywine but remains surprisingly smooth.

This is absolutely a beer to sip rather than consume like a conventional pint.

---

# Finish

Long, warming, and malt-driven.

Caramel and vanilla remain prominent while the darker oak character gradually becomes more noticeable.

The alcohol provides warmth without completely taking over the finish.

---

# What I Liked

* Exceptional sipping beer

# What I'd Change

The sweetness is substantial, so this is not a beer for every occasion.

At 15% ABV, the beer also demands patience. Drinking it too quickly would likely make the alcohol much more noticeable and would miss much of the layered character.

For me, however, those are more characteristics of the style than significant flaws.

---

# Comparison Notes

Straight Jacket sits in an interesting place within my collection because it shares characteristics with both **barleywine and bourbon**.

The combination of:

* Caramel
* Vanilla
* Dark fruit
* Sweet malt
* Oak
* Alcohol warmth

reminds me of the sensory progression I enjoy in bourbon.

Compared with lighter or more highly carbonated Belgian strong ales, Straight Jacket is considerably heavier and more contemplative.

It is closer to a **dessert-like bourbon tasting experience in beer form** than a conventional beer-drinking experience.

---

# Final Thoughts

Straight Jacket remains one of my favorite high-ABV beers because it understands how to use alcohol rather than simply hide it.

At 15%, the beer is powerful, but the experience is driven by **caramel, vanilla, sweet malt, creamy texture, and layered oak** rather than raw alcohol.

The most interesting characteristic is the **dark oak hiding underneath the sweeter flavors**. It gives the beer a deeper character that becomes easier to notice as it opens.

For my palate, this is exactly what a high-ABV barleywine should be:

**rich, layered, warming, creamy, and meant to be sipped slowly.**
