# Why I Bought It

Willettized is one of those beers that you don't necessarily see every day.

You have to keep an eye out for it.

When it shows up, I pay attention.

Lagunitas took a special coffee-infused Imperial Stout and aged it in Bourbon and Rye barrels from Willett Distillery in Kentucky.

That combination immediately puts this beer in my wheelhouse.

I've always considered myself a Belgian and Imperial Stout guy, and this is the type of beer that reminds me why.

It's rich.

It's dark.

It's coffee-forward.

It's chocolate-driven.

And then the barrel brings in another layer of bourbon, vanilla, oak, and time.

This is not a beer I expect to find sitting around everywhere.

It's a **hunt bottle**.

And when I find it, I'm buying it.

# Appearance

Deep, dark, and imposing.

This is an Imperial Stout that looks like an Imperial Stout should look.

The beer pours nearly black with a rich, dark appearance that immediately signals roasted malt, chocolate, and barrel character.

---

# Aroma

Coffee and chocolate lead the way.

Then the barrel starts to show itself.

There is bourbon character underneath the roast, along with vanilla and oak.

The overall aroma is rich without becoming overly sweet.

It's the kind of beer where you can sit with the glass for a while and keep finding something new.

Coffee.

Dark chocolate.

Roasted malt.

Bourbon.

Vanilla.

Oak.

A little smoke.

Everything working together.

---

# Flavor

This is where Willettized earns its place.

The coffee and chocolate balance is excellent.

The coffee brings roast and depth without completely overwhelming the chocolate.

The chocolate gives the beer richness and sweetness.

Then the Willett barrels add another dimension.

Bourbon.

Vanilla.

Oak.

Time.

The barrel character doesn't feel like it's simply sitting on top of the stout.

It becomes part of the beer.

The result is a complex Imperial Stout that moves through several layers as you drink it.

First the roasted coffee.

Then chocolate.

Then bourbon and vanilla.

Then the darker barrel and oak character.

It's rich, but the coffee and roast keep it from becoming nothing but a sugar bomb.

---

# Mouthfeel

Big.

Rich.

Viscous.

Creamy.

This is a beer that has weight.

The barrel aging adds to the experience rather than taking away from the underlying stout.

It feels like a beer you should slow down with.

This isn't a six-pack crusher.

This is a glass-pour beer.

Take your time.

Let it warm slightly.

See what changes.

---

# Finish

Long and complex.

The coffee and chocolate remain present while the bourbon barrel character continues to develop.

Vanilla and oak linger underneath the roast.

There is a subtle smoky quality that adds another layer.

The finish encourages you to take another sip, but this is definitely a beer to drink slowly.

The goal isn't to finish it quickly.

The goal is to experience it.

---

# What I Liked

- Excellent coffee and chocolate balance.
- Bourbon barrel character is integrated into the stout.
- Willett Bourbon and Rye barrels add serious depth.
- Vanilla character from the toasted barrels.
- Dark chocolate malt character.
- Coffee adds complexity without completely dominating.
- Rich and viscous Imperial Stout body.
- Complex enough to keep exploring with every sip.
- Feels like a true barrel-aged stout rather than a stout with bourbon flavor added afterward.

Most importantly:

**It tastes like a serious Imperial Stout first and a barrel-aged beer second.**

That's important to me.

The barrel should enhance the beer.

It shouldn't erase what made the base beer good.

Willettized gets that balance right.

# What I'd Change

This isn't necessarily a beer I can just decide to buy whenever I want.

# Comparison Notes

Willettized belongs in the same conversation as Bourbon County-style barrel-aged stouts.

The profile is familiar:

- Imperial Stout
- Coffee
- Chocolate
- Bourbon
- Oak
- Vanilla
- Barrel aging

But Willettized has its own identity.

The use of Willett Bourbon and Rye barrels gives it a distinctive character, while the coffee and chocolate profile keeps the beer firmly rooted in Imperial Stout territory.

---

Revolution's barrel-aged and Imperial Stout offerings are another natural comparison.

**Big Stout + Big Barrel + Big Flavor.**

For me, Willettized can absolutely compete in that space.
