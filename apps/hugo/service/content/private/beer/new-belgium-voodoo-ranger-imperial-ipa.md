# Appearance

A rich golden-amber color with enough depth to remind you that this is a 9% Imperial IPA.

It pours with a substantial presence but doesn't look excessively heavy or dark.

The appearance fits the beer perfectly: somewhere between bright hop-driven IPA and a fuller-bodied Double IPA.

---

# Aroma

The nose brings the classic combination of citrus and pine.

Fresh-cut pine sits alongside bright citrus hop character, giving the beer an immediately recognizable West Coast-inspired presence.

The hops are clearly there, but the aroma isn't trying to overwhelm you.

---

# Flavor

This is where Voodoo Ranger Imperial IPA separates itself from Maximus.

The hops arrive clearly, but they are balanced by a lighter malt profile.

Where Maximus is more malt-forward, creamy, and full-bodied, Voodoo Ranger feels more balanced and hop-driven without becoming aggressively bitter.

The citrus and pine character comes through cleanly.

There is enough malt underneath to give the beer structure, but it doesn't dominate the experience.

The result is a DIPA that manages to be:

- Hoppy
- Smooth
- Balanced
- Flavorful
- Surprisingly easy to drink for 9% ABV

This is about as smooth as I want a Double IPA to get without losing the character that makes a DIPA a DIPA.

---

# Mouthfeel

Medium-bodied with a smooth and creamy character.

Compared with Lagunitas Maximus, Voodoo Ranger Imperial IPA feels lighter and less malt-forward.

That lighter body makes it easier to drink and gives the hops more room to shine.

It's not thin.

It's simply more balanced.

---

# Finish

The finish is smooth with lingering hop character and a clean bitterness.

The bitterness is present but doesn't dominate the beer.

This is one of the things I appreciate most about it.

The hops don't beat you over the head.

They stay integrated with the malt and continue to develop as you drink.

---

# What I Liked

- 9% ABV without drinking overly hot
- Excellent balance between hops and malt
- Citrus and pine hop profile
- Smooth and creamy mouthfeel
- Less malt-forward than Lagunitas Maximus
- Hops are present without becoming obnoxious
- Very drinkable for a Double IPA
- Consistent and dependable
- Excellent value at $14.99 for a 12-pack

---

# What I'd Change

For my personal taste, I wouldn't change much.

If anything, I could see wanting just a little more body.

This is where Maximus still wins for me.

I enjoy a malt-forward Imperial IPA with a thicker, creamier body, and Maximus delivers more of that experience.

Voodoo Ranger takes a more balanced approach.

That isn't a flaw—it's simply the reason I reach for each beer differently.

# Final Thoughts

Voodoo Ranger Imperial IPA is a damn good Double IPA.

For my personal taste, I still put Lagunitas Maximus ahead of it because I prefer the fuller body, creamier mouthfeel, and stronger malt presence.

But Voodoo Ranger does something different—and does it extremely well.

It's lighter on the malt, more balanced, and lets the hops take a more prominent role without becoming aggressive.

The 9% ABV is well integrated.

The citrus and pine character is classic.

The bitterness is smooth.

The body is substantial enough to avoid feeling thin, but light enough to remain highly drinkable.

At $14.99 for a 12-pack, this becomes even harder to argue with.

In a market where beer prices keep creeping upward, beers like this are becoming increasingly important.

You don't always need the newest limited release.

Sometimes you just need a damn good beer that you can buy again.

Voodoo Ranger Imperial IPA is that beer for me.
